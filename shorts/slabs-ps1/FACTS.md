# FACTS — One-Way vs Two-Way Slabs (PS1 cat)

| Claim in film | Source | Quote / note |
|---|---|---|
| Tributary area = the floor area that loads a beam; one-way slabs send load in one direction, two-way slabs to all edges | CEE159 Class 03 slides (Reis & Pessiki, Lehigh, Fall 2026) | "Tributary Area is the amount of area that contributes to loading a beam or column … one-way: loads are distributed in one direction (lateral edges) · two-way: loads are distributed in two directions (all edges)" |
| Long ÷ short > 2 → one-way; ≤ 2 → two-way | Hibbeler, *Structural Analysis* §2.2 (course text), as summarized in https://www.transtutors.com/questions/if-according-to-the-aci-318-concrete-code-the-support-ratio-in-fig-2-12b-is-l-2-l-1--2643361.htm and https://skyciv.com/el/technical/area-loads-in-one-way-and-two-way-systems/ | ACI 318 convention used in the text |
| Panel sizes 18 × 6 ft and 12 × 9 ft, 400 lb/ft² | CEE159 Class 04 examples (a = 6 ft, b = 18 ft; a = 9 ft, b = 12 ft; "uniform live loading of 400 lb/ft²") | The film uses one isolated panel on four beams (simplified from the lecture's framework) |
| Two-way: 45° lines from each corner → triangles on short beams, trapezoids on long beams | https://skyciv.com/el/technical/area-loads-in-one-way-and-two-way-systems/ ; https://civilengineeronline.com/str/tributory-load.php | |
| One-way beam load = 400 × (6/2) = 1,200 lb/ft (uniform); two-way peak = 400 × (9/2) = 1,800 lb/ft on both beam types | computed from the above for a single panel | Tributary width = half the short span |

The frame, slab and cat are an illustrative low-poly model; the bar heights are drawn to scale with the tributary width.
