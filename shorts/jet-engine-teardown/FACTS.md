# Jet engine (turbofan): facts and sources

Facts come from search-result text of the pages below (direct page fetches are limited on this network).

| Claim in script | Source(s) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Turbofans power almost every airliner | Wikipedia "Turbofan" (IPFS mirror); globeair.com "High-Bypass Turbofan" | Modern airliners use high-bypass turbofans. |
| Job: move a large mass of air and accelerate it rearward; thrust = reaction (Newton's 3rd law) | Wikipedia "Turbofan" | Standard momentum explanation. |
| Most intake air bypasses the core; bypass air makes most of the thrust | USPTO patent 4142365 (at bypass ratio 6, ~85 % of thrust from the fan duct); rotatepilot.com (75–85 % for BPR 5–12) | Wikipedia gives a wider 30–70 % range across all turbofans; "most" is stated for high-bypass engines like the CFM56-7B (BPR ~5). |
| Compressor: rows of blades, stage after stage; CFM56-7B = 3-stage booster + 9-stage HPC | Aircraft Commerce Issue 58 "CFM56-7B guide"; Embry-Riddle CFM56-7B notes (Scribd) | Shown on label as "3 + 9 stages". |
| "Over 30 times outside pressure" | Univ. Blida CFM56-7B course document: overall pressure ratio 32.3–32.6 at max climb | Kept as "over 30×". |
| Combustor burns fuel continuously | Wikipedia "Turbofan" / gas-turbine basics | Continuous (Brayton) combustion, unlike a piston engine. "Blowtorch" is an analogy. |
| Turbine drives compressor and fan through shafts (two spools); CFM56-7B = 1-stage HPT + 4-stage LPT | Aircraft Commerce Issue 58 | HP spool: HPC + HPT; LP spool: fan + booster + LPT. |
| Nozzle: fast jet out the back → engine pushed forward | Wikipedia "Turbofan" | |
| CFM56-7B powers the Boeing 737 (Next Generation) | planefyi.com "CFM56" (737-800: -7B26/27; 737-900ER: -7B27A) | |
| Fan ~61 in (1.55 m) → "five-foot fan" | Aircraft Commerce Issue 58 specs; Purdue AAE "CFM56 family" | 61 in ≈ 5.1 ft. |
| Bypass ratio ~5:1 ("about five times more air around the core") | Aircraft Commerce Issue 58: 5.5 (lowest rating) to 5.1 (highest) | |
| Up to 27,300 lbf ("up to 27,000 pounds of thrust") | planefyi.com: -7B27 / -7B27A rated 27,300 lbf | Range ~19,500–27,300 lbf for 737NG ratings. |
