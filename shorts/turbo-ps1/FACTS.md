# FACTS — How A Turbo Works (PS1 style)

| Claim in film | Source | Quote / note |
|---|---|---|
| A turbo is a compressor driven by a turbine powered by engine exhaust, joined by a common shaft | ME121 "Performance of a Turbocharger System" handout (Lehigh, FA26) | "essentially a compressor that is operated by a turbine that is driven by engine exhaust gases … connected by a common shaft" |
| The compressor raises intake pressure; that rise is called boost | same handout | "The pressure increase effected by the turbocharger compressor is termed the 'boost'" |
| Compressing air also heats it, which cuts density; an intercooler cools it before the cylinders | same handout | "simultaneously increasing the temperature tends to negate some of the density increase … an intercooler can be used after the compressor outlet" |
| More air mass lets the engine burn more fuel → more work | same handout ; Garrett Turbo Tech 101 https://www.garrettmotion.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Turbo-Tech-101.pdf | "With more air mass, comes more fuel & hence more work output per power cycle" ; "Engine power is proportional to the amount of air and fuel that can get into the cylinders" |
| It isn't free: part of the engine's output goes into driving the turbo | same handout | "a portion of that output is consumed to operate the turbocharger" |

The turbo, engine, intercooler and mascot cat are an illustrative low-poly model. The power bar is a diagram, not measured data.
