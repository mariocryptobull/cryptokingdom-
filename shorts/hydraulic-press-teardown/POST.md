# Post: How a Hydraulic Press Works

**Caption**
How does a hydraulic press crush almost anything? 🔩 Pascal's law: squeeze trapped oil and the pressure is the same everywhere, so force = pressure × area. A 7/16" pump piston (0.15 in²) and a 2¼" ram (4 in²) give 26× the force: a ~90 lb pull on the handle becomes 10,000 psi and 40,000 lb on the ram. Twenty tons. The catch: each pump stroke moves the ram about 1 mm. A soda can gives up at ~200 lb.

Sources: Enerpac P-392 hand pump specs (0.151 in³/stroke, 10,000 psi, 93 lb handle effort); US patents 4290354 & 12384590 (can axial strength); hydraulic jack explainers (Tameson). Derived numbers shown in the video. Schematic model, not to scale.
