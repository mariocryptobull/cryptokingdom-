# Holland Tunnel ventilation: facts and sources

Direct page fetches are blocked on this network (ethw.org, wikipedia.org did not resolve); facts come from search-result text of the pages below.

| Claim in script | Source(s) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opened 1927 | Wikipedia "Holland Tunnel"; Structurae; Port Authority "The Holland at 90" | Opened to traffic Nov 13, 1927 (ceremony Nov 12). |
| Over a mile and a half under the Hudson, NJ to Manhattan; 8,558 ft tube | PBS Building Big "Holland Tunnel": 8,558 ft (north tube), 8,371 ft (south tube); Wikipedia infobox same | 8,558 ft = 1.62 mi. Historical marker gives ~9,250 ft total incl. approaches. |
| Carbon monoxide was the danger; tests at Yale and the U.S. Bureau of Mines: > 4 parts in 10,000 dangerous | Time (1927) "Science: Holland Tunnel": experiments at Yale, Univ. of Illinois and the U.S. Bureau of Mines showed "more than four parts of the gas in 10,000 of air was dangerous"; News-Gazette "Yale, UI teamed up on tunnel ventilation system" | Pittsburgh Quarterly cites up to 6 parts tolerable; the 4-part figure is the one most repeated. Bureau of Mines measured car emissions at Bruceton; Yale ran human exposure tests. |
| Chief engineer Clifford Holland died 1924, before it opened | Wikipedia; Untapped Cities "10 secrets of the Holland Tunnel" | Holland (1883–1924); tunnel named for him. |
| Ole Singstad designed the ventilation and finished the tunnel | Wikipedia: "world's first mechanically ventilated tunnel, with a ventilation system designed by Ole Singstad, who oversaw the tunnel's completion" | Script simplifies: Milton Freeman briefly succeeded Holland and also died; Singstad then completed it. |
| Transverse ventilation: fresh air blown into a duct under the roadway, enters through slots at the curb; exhaust drawn up through ceiling openings into a duct above the ceiling and out through the vent building | ASME landmark #93 "Holland Tunnel Ventilation System" (ETHW) | Shown schematically; shafts drawn in the cut plane. |
| Four ventilation buildings: two on land, two in the river | Structurae: each side has one on land and one in the river ~1,000 ft from shore; ASME: two in NJ, two in NY | |
| 84 fans: 42 blowers, 42 exhaust | ASME/ETHW; Port Authority | Not all run at once (Time: 56 needed, 28 reserve). |
| Can replace all the tunnel air every 90 seconds | ASME/ETHW ("completely change the tunnel air every 90 seconds"); Untapped Cities | At full speed; on-screen card says "FULL SPEED". |
| World's first mechanically ventilated tunnel | Wikipedia; Structurae | |
| National Historic Landmark (1993) | Wikipedia; Structurae | Also ASCE/ASME National Historic Civil & Mechanical Engineering Landmark (1982 per Wikipedia, plaque 1984 per ASME). |
