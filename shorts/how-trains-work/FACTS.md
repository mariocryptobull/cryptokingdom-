# FACTS — How Train Systems Operate

| Claim in film | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Catenary between New Haven and Boston is 25 kV, 60 Hz | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northend_Electrification_Project ("Amtrak's 60 Hz traction power system") | The NEC also runs 12 kV 25 Hz south of New York, so the film names the segment. |
| Pantograph pulls power from the overhead wire | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantograph_(transport) | General mechanism |
| Tapered (conical) wheel treads give passive steering on curves | https://railroads.dot.gov/sites/fra.dot.gov/files/fra_net/2071/rr0820.pdf ; https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/14622/1/paper.pdf | "the wheelset's tendency to steer into a curve, driven by the taper and lateral shift" |
| PTC uses GPS, digital radio, and onboard computers, and can stop a train automatically | https://railroads.dot.gov/sites/fra.dot.gov/files/2019-11/FRA%20Positive%20Train%20Control%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf | Prevents train-to-train collisions, overspeed derailments, and work-zone incursions |
| PTC was mandated by the Rail Safety Improvement Act of 2008 | https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20180904_R42637_89475c3cc29604ac820025e011f41ebb8606c5d9.html | |
| All railroads required to implement PTC had done so by 31 Dec 2020 | https://railroads.fra.dot.gov/sites/fra.dot.gov/files/2022-12/2022_12%20PTC%20FAQs_final.pdf | Per FRA |
| Regenerative braking: motors act as generators | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regenerative_brake | General mechanism; no efficiency number is shown |

No dimensions appear on screen apart from the sourced 25 kV. The train geometry is an illustrative model, and the film says so.
